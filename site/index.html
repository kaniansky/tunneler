TUNNELER

by Norko Kaniansky

Two tanks under the ground. Dig through the dirt, find the other tank and shoot it before your energy runs out. Geoffrey Silverton's DOS game from 1991, rebuilt from its decompiled code so it plays exactly as it did, and extended to eight tanks, teams, computer opponents and online games.

New in 6.0

Three new ways to play: quick play puts you in an online game in seconds, the daily challenge gives everybody the same game for a day, with a leaderboard, and the map editor lets you draw your own worlds. Online play now uses free player accounts, and online games carry on through server restarts.

Quick play

One choice on the main menu and you are in an online game. The server finds a game with a place for you or starts one: four tanks, everyone for themselves, computer tanks in every seat nobody sits on, and matches that start by themselves after a short countdown. Join a match that is already running and you take over a computer tank at once; leave, and the computer drives it again.

Daily challenge

Every day a new skirmish, the same for everyone: the same world, mode, map size and computer opponents. Play it as often as you like and send your best result. The server plays your match again before it goes on the day's leaderboard, so only real results count.

Watch the match behind any entry on the board. Go back through the 30 days before to see their boards and play their challenges again for practice.

Your own maps

Paint a world of rock, dirt and tunnels and place up to eight bases, on a small, medium or large map, starting from plain dirt or a random world. The editor works with the keyboard, the mouse, a gamepad and on a phone. Keep your maps in the browser, share them as links or save them as .tmap files, and play them in a skirmish or an online game.

One rule: a tank must be able to drive between all the bases. The editor says which base can't be reached, and the map can't be played until you dig a way.

Also in 6.0

  • Player accounts for online games: a username (the name everybody sees, yours alone on that server) and a password, no e-mail. A recovery code, shown once when you sign up, is how you set a new password. Your account is you: your seat or tank comes back on any device, and nobody else can play under your name. Split screen, skirmish, replays, maps and looking at the daily challenge need no account.
  • Running reliably: a server restart or an update no longer ends online games. They move to the new server, and the players' pages reconnect and carry on. A dropped connection (a phone changing networks, a laptop waking up) reconnects within seconds with your tank, and a desync repairs itself.
  • Realistic sound is the default style now. The original PC speaker is one choice away in Settings → Sound.

Since 5.0

Modern graphics are the default: the same game drawn at your screen's resolution, with lit tunnels, dust and sparks, while the original DOS look is one switch away in Settings → Graphics.

All changes, version by version

Three ways to play

Split screen

Two players on one keyboard, as in 1991. BLUE on the left steers with W A S D, GREEN on the right with the arrow keys. On the medium map with three rounds to win, this is the original game, frame for frame.

Skirmish

Up to eight tanks: you, and a friend if you like, against computer tanks at easy, medium or hard. Any teams, from everyone for themselves to four against four.

Online

Quick play finds you a game at once; or create a game and send the invite link. Each game is a lobby with eight seats (open, closed or taken by a computer tank) with teams, chat and the host's settings. Others can watch as spectators. Online play needs a free account on the server.

Game modes

A skirmish or an online game can be played in any of these. All of them use the original's tanks, digging and shots.

standard
The original rules: the last tank or team still standing wins the round.
gold rush
Gold nuggets lie hidden in the dirt. Bring them home to your base; a hit makes you drop one.
infection
One tank starts infected, and the healthy tanks it destroys come back infected. Stay healthy until the time runs out.
survival
All players together against waves of computer tanks, each wave bigger and tougher, up to ten.
king of the hill
Hold the zone in the middle of the map. The first team to hold it long enough wins.
shrinking world
The safe area shrinks every 30 seconds. Outside it, your shield drains away.
capture the flag
Take the enemy's flag and bring it to your base while your own flag is at home.
base siege
Bases can be shot down. The last team with a base or a tank left wins.
deathmatch
Destroyed tanks come back after three seconds. The first team to the kill target wins.

Settings for every game: three map sizes (the original, half and double) or a map of your own, 1 to 9 rounds to win, a round time limit, friendly fire for teams, line of sight, and what you watch after your tank is destroyed.

The 1991 game, exactly

TUNNELER 2.0 was written by Geoffrey Silverton in Turbo Pascal in 1991 and released into the public domain. This version starts from its decompiled code and follows it routine by routine: the same random number generator called in the same order, the same 16-bit arithmetic, even the original's bugs.

An automated test runs the original program next to this version with the same keys pressed and compares every frame: the world, the tanks, the shots and the screen. Everything beyond 1991 (more tanks, teams, modes, online play, the modern look) is built on top of that.

How to play

Each tank starts in its own base. Dig through the dirt (rock can't be dug), find the enemy and shoot it. Moving costs energy, digging costs more, and shooting the most. Any base refuels you; your own base repairs your shield, and a teammate's does too, more slowly. Firing while you move digs faster. Win the set number of rounds to win the match.

Download

A small desktop app: the game in its own window, using the web view your system already has, so it is only a few MB. Split screen and skirmish work offline; online play uses the public server or your own. The app tells you when a new version is out.

Latest release: GitHub

Windows

tunneler-…-windows-x64-setup.exe / .exe

Windows 10 / 11. The setup installs it and keeps it up to date; the plain .exe just runs.

Linux

.AppImage / .deb / .rpm / .tar.gz

Needs WebKitGTK 4.1 (most desktops have it). The AppImage: make it executable first (chmod +x, or Properties → Allow executing); it updates itself. The .deb / .rpm update with the admin password, or use the repository below.

macOS

.dmg (Apple silicon / Intel)

Not signed yet: right click → Open the first time. Updates itself.

Android

.apk

Android 7 or newer, phones and tablets, with on-screen controls. Allow installing from your browser the first time; a new version is a new download.

Linux repository

Debian, Ubuntu, Mint: add the repository once and the game updates with the rest of your system.

curl -fsSL https://kaniansky.com/tunneler/tunneler.gpg | sudo tee /usr/share/keyrings/tunneler.gpg >/dev/null
echo "deb [signed-by=/usr/share/keyrings/tunneler.gpg] https://kaniansky.com/tunneler/apt ./" | sudo tee /etc/apt/sources.list.d/tunneler.list
sudo apt update && sudo apt install tunneler

Fedora and other dnf systems:

sudo curl -fsSL -o /etc/yum.repos.d/tunneler.repo https://kaniansky.com/tunneler/rpm/tunneler.repo
sudo dnf install tunneler

Your own server

Prefer to host it yourself? The server is one Node.js file or a Docker image:

docker run -p 8050:8050 ghcr.io/kaniansky/tunneler

Players need an account on your server too (a username and a password, kept by the server); ACCOUNTS=off runs it with free names instead, for example on a LAN. How to keep accounts and games through restarts: Hosting in the README.

Privacy

The game has no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Online games and the daily challenge's leaderboard need an account on the game server: a username and a password, nothing else (no e-mail). Game rooms (names, chat, moves) stay in the server's memory only while a game exists. The apps ask GitHub whether a new version is out, and connect to a game server only when you play online.
